UCP 600 · ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits

Documentary credits

The ICC rules used in most documentary credits worldwide. They apply when the credit states that it is subject to UCP 600. Summaries here are in my own words; always check the official ICC text.

Based on ICC Publication No. 600 · Last reviewed October 2026

What is a letter of credit?

An LC is a bank's promise to pay the seller, provided the seller presents documents that comply with the credit's terms. The bank looks only at the documents, never at the goods. That single idea explains most of how LCs work.

  1. Buyer ↔ SellerSale contractParties agree that payment will be by LC.
  2. Applicant → Issuing bankLC applicationBuyer asks its bank to issue the credit.
  3. Issuing bank → Advising bankIssuanceLC is sent, usually by SWIFT MT700.
  4. Advising bank → BeneficiaryAdviceSeller's bank checks authenticity and advises it.
  5. BeneficiaryShipmentSeller ships goods and gathers the documents.
  6. Beneficiary → Nominated bankPresentationDocuments are presented within the period and expiry.
  7. BanksExaminationUp to 5 banking days to decide if documents comply.
  8. Issuing bank → BeneficiaryPaymentComplying documents are honoured; buyer reimburses.
ApplicantThe buyer who requests the credit.
BeneficiaryThe seller who gets paid.

All 39 articles

General provisions

Articles 1–5
Art. 1Application of UCP
The rules apply when the credit expressly says it is subject to them. They bind all parties unless the credit modifies or excludes a rule. They can also apply to standby credits, as far as they fit.
Art. 2Definitions
Defines the core terms: advising bank, applicant, beneficiary, banking day, complying presentation, confirmation, honour, negotiation, nominated bank, presentation and presenter. Honour means paying at sight, giving a deferred payment undertaking, or accepting a draft. Negotiation means the nominated bank buying drafts or documents by advancing or agreeing to advance funds.
Art. 3Interpretations
Sets reading rules. A credit is irrevocable even if it does not say so. A signature can be handwritten, stamped, symbolic or electronic. Vague issuer terms such as 'first class' or 'competent' allow any issuer except the beneficiary. 'On or about' means five days either side. 'To', 'until', 'from' and 'between' include the named date for shipment; 'before' and 'after' exclude it. First half and second half of a month mean the 1st–15th and 16th–last day; beginning, middle and end mean the 1st–10th, 11th–20th and 21st–last day. Branches in different countries count as separate banks.
Art. 4Credits v. contracts
A credit is a separate transaction from the sale contract. Banks are not bound by the contract even if the credit refers to it. Issuing banks should discourage applicants from attaching contracts or proforma invoices to the credit.
Art. 5Documents v. goods
Banks deal with documents only, not with the goods, services or performance the documents relate to.

Liabilities and obligations

Articles 6–13
Art. 6Availability, expiry date and place for presentation
The credit must name the bank it is available with (or say any bank), how it is available (sight payment, deferred payment, acceptance or negotiation) and an expiry date. A credit available with a nominated bank is also available with the issuing bank. It must not be available by a draft drawn on the applicant. Presentation must be made on or before the expiry date.
Art. 7Issuing bank undertaking
The issuing bank must honour a complying presentation and is irrevocably bound from the moment it issues the credit. It must also reimburse a nominated bank that has honoured or negotiated a complying presentation, at maturity for deferred payment or acceptance credits, whether or not that bank paid early.
Art. 8Confirming bank undertaking
A confirming bank must honour, or negotiate without recourse, a complying presentation, and is irrevocably bound from the moment it adds confirmation. A bank asked to confirm but unwilling to must tell the issuing bank without delay, and may still advise the credit without confirmation.
Art. 9Advising of credits and amendments
An advising bank gives no undertaking to pay. By advising, it signals that it is satisfied the credit looks authentic and that the advice reflects the terms accurately. A bank that will not advise, or cannot check authenticity, must say so without delay to the bank that sent it.
Art. 10Amendments
A credit cannot be amended or cancelled without the agreement of the issuing bank, any confirming bank and the beneficiary. The original terms stay in force for the beneficiary until it accepts the amendment. A presentation that complies with the amendment counts as acceptance. Partial acceptance is not allowed and counts as rejection. A clause saying an amendment takes effect unless rejected within a set time is disregarded.
Art. 11Teletransmitted and pre-advised credits and amendments
An authenticated teletransmission (such as SWIFT) is the operative credit, and any later mail confirmation is disregarded. If it says 'full details to follow', it is not operative until those details arrive. A bank that sends a pre-advice must then issue the operative credit without delay, on terms consistent with the pre-advice.
Art. 12Nomination
Being nominated does not oblige a bank to honour or negotiate unless it agrees or is the confirming bank. Nominating a bank to accept a draft or incur a deferred payment undertaking authorises it to prepay or buy that draft or undertaking. Receiving or forwarding documents does not by itself make a nominated bank liable.
Art. 13Bank-to-bank reimbursement arrangements
If the credit says reimbursement is subject to the ICC reimbursement rules (URR), those apply. Otherwise, the issuing bank must give the reimbursing bank a proper authorisation, and a claiming bank cannot be asked for a certificate of compliance. If reimbursement is not made on first demand, the issuing bank remains liable, including for any lost interest.

Examination of documents

Articles 14–17
Art. 14Standard for examination of documents
Banks examine documents alone to decide whether they appear to comply. Each bank has up to five banking days after the day of presentation. A presentation that includes original transport documents (Arts. 19–25) must be made within 21 calendar days after shipment, unless the credit sets a different period, and in any case no later than the expiry date. Data need not be identical across documents but must not conflict. Outside the invoice, goods may be described in general terms. Documents not called for are disregarded, and conditions with no stated document are ignored. Addresses need not match the credit exactly but must be in the same country. The shipper need not be the beneficiary.
Art. 15Complying presentation
When the presentation complies, the issuing bank must honour, a confirming bank must honour or negotiate and forward the documents, and a nominated bank that honours or negotiates must forward them to the confirming or issuing bank.
Art. 16Discrepant documents, waiver and notice
A bank may refuse documents that do not comply. The issuing bank may ask the applicant to waive discrepancies, but this does not extend the examination period. A refusal must be a single notice, sent by the end of the fifth banking day after presentation, stating each discrepancy and what the bank is doing with the documents. A bank that fails to follow this cannot later claim the documents were discrepant.
Art. 17Original documents and copies
At least one original of each required document must be presented. A document with an apparently original signature, mark, stamp or label of its issuer is treated as an original. Where copies are asked for, copies or originals are acceptable. Terms like 'in duplicate' are met by one original and the rest copies.

Documents

Articles 18–28
Art. 18Commercial invoice
The invoice must appear to be issued by the beneficiary, be made out in the applicant's name, and be in the credit's currency. It need not be signed. The goods description must correspond with the credit. A bank may accept an invoice above the credit amount, as long as it does not pay more than the credit allows.
Art. 19Transport document covering at least two different modes
For multimodal shipments, the document must name the carrier and be signed by the carrier, master or a named agent. It must show dispatch, taking in charge or shipment at the place named in the credit, and the final destination. The issue date is treated as the shipment date unless a dated notation says otherwise. Transhipment may be shown as long as one document covers the whole carriage.
Art. 20Bill of lading
The bill must name the carrier and be signed by the carrier, master or a named agent. It must show goods shipped on board a named vessel at the port of loading in the credit, by pre-printed wording or a dated on-board notation. Ports must match the credit, and the full set of originals must be presented. It must not indicate a charter party. Transhipment may be shown if one bill covers the whole voyage, even where the credit prohibits it, if goods are in containers, trailers or LASH barges.
Art. 21Non-negotiable sea waybill
Follows the same requirements as the bill of lading: named carrier, signature, on-board shipment at the right port, correct ports and no charter party indication.
Art. 22Charter party bill of lading
Must be signed by the master, owner or charterer, or an agent for them, and show goods shipped on board at the port of loading in the credit. The port of discharge may be shown as a range or area if the credit allows. Banks do not examine the charter party contract itself, even if it is presented.
Art. 23Air transport document
Must name the carrier and be signed by the carrier or a named agent, and show the goods were accepted for carriage. The issue date counts as the shipment date unless the document carries a specific notation of the actual shipment date. Airports must match the credit. The original for consignor or shipper is enough, even if the credit asks for a full set.
Art. 24Road, rail or inland waterway transport documents
Must name the carrier and be signed or show receipt by the carrier or its agent, and show the places of shipment and destination stated in the credit. A road document must appear to be the original for consignor or shipper; a rail document marked 'duplicate' is accepted as an original.
Art. 25Courier receipt, post receipt or certificate of posting
A courier receipt must name the courier and be stamped or signed by it, with a pick-up or receipt date that counts as the shipment date. A post receipt must be stamped or signed at the place from which the credit says goods are to be shipped.
Art. 26'On deck', 'shipper's load and count' and charges additional to freight
A transport document must not show goods loaded on deck, though saying they may be loaded on deck is fine. Clauses such as 'shipper's load and count' or 'said by shipper to contain' are acceptable, as is a reference to charges in addition to freight.
Art. 27Clean transport document
Banks accept only clean transport documents, meaning no clause or notation expressly declaring that the goods or their packaging are defective. The word 'clean' need not appear on the document.
Art. 28Insurance document and coverage
Must be issued and signed by an insurer, underwriter or their agent, with all originals presented. Cover notes are not accepted. It must be dated no later than shipment unless cover starts from shipment, be in the credit's currency, and cover at least 110% of the CIF or CIP value if the credit is silent. Cover must run between the places stated in the credit. It may show a franchise or excess.

Miscellaneous provisions

Articles 29–33
Art. 29Extension of expiry date or last day for presentation
If the expiry date or last day for presentation falls on a day the bank is closed for reasons other than force majeure, it moves to the next banking day. The nominated bank must state that presentation was made within the extended time. The latest shipment date is never extended.
Art. 30Tolerance in credit amount, quantity and unit prices
'About' or 'approximately' allows 10% more or less on the amount, quantity or unit price it refers to. If quantity is not stated in packing units or individual items, a 5% quantity tolerance applies, as long as the amount is not exceeded. When the full quantity is shipped and unit price is not reduced, a drawing up to 5% below the credit amount is allowed, even if partial shipments are prohibited.
Art. 31Partial drawings or shipments
Partial drawings and shipments are allowed unless the credit says otherwise. Several transport documents for shipment on the same vessel and journey to the same destination are not partial shipments, even with different dates or ports of loading; the latest date counts as the shipment date. Shipment on more than one means of conveyance is a partial shipment.
Art. 32Instalment drawings or shipments
If the credit sets a schedule of instalments and any instalment is missed, the credit stops being available for that instalment and all later ones.
Art. 33Hours of presentation
A bank does not have to accept a presentation outside its banking hours.

Disclaimers

Articles 34–37
Art. 34Disclaimer on effectiveness of documents
Banks are not responsible for the form, accuracy, genuineness or legal effect of any document, nor for the description, quantity, quality or condition of the goods, nor for the good faith or acts of any party.
Art. 35Disclaimer on transmission and translation
Banks are not liable for delay, loss or errors in transmitting messages or documents sent as the credit requires. If a nominated bank finds the documents comply and forwards them, the issuing or confirming bank must honour even if the documents are lost in transit. Banks are not liable for translation errors.
Art. 36Force majeure
Banks are not liable for interruptions caused by events beyond their control, such as natural disasters, riots, war, terrorism or strikes. A credit that expires during such an interruption is not revived when the bank reopens.
Art. 37Disclaimer for acts of an instructed party
A bank that uses another bank to carry out the applicant's instructions does so at the applicant's cost and risk. If the credit puts charges on the beneficiary and they cannot be collected, the issuing bank remains liable for them. The applicant must indemnify banks against obligations imposed by foreign laws and usages.

Transfer and assignment

Articles 38–39
Art. 38Transferable credits
Only a credit expressly marked 'transferable' can be transferred, and the transferring bank need not agree beyond what it has consented to. It can be transferred once, in whole or part, to one or more second beneficiaries if partial drawings are allowed, but a second beneficiary cannot transfer it again. The transferred credit may reduce the amount, unit price, expiry, presentation period and shipment dates, and increase the insurance percentage. The first beneficiary may substitute its own invoice and draft.
Art. 39Assignment of proceeds
Even if a credit is not transferable, the beneficiary may assign the proceeds under applicable law. This covers only the money, not the right to perform under the credit.

Art.

Reading an MT700

An LC usually arrives as a SWIFT MT700. These are the fields I check first, and what to look for in each.

40E
Applicable rules

Shows which rules govern the credit. For a standard documentary credit this is usually 'UCP LATEST VERSION'. Check that it matches what was agreed. Other options include eUCP or ISP98, and the credit must then be read under those rules.

31D
Date and place of expiry

Documents must reach the place named here by this date. An expiry at the issuing bank abroad leaves you less time than one in your own country.

32B
Currency and amount

The invoice and draft cannot exceed this, beyond any tolerance in 39A.

39A
Amount tolerance

For example 10/10 allows 10% more or less. If blank, only the Art. rules apply.

41a
Available with … by …

Which bank pays or negotiates, and whether by sight payment, deferred payment, acceptance or negotiation.

42C
Drafts at

The tenor of the draft: at sight, or a number of days after sight or shipment. Draft wording must match.

43P / 43T
Partial shipments and transhipment

Allowed or not allowed. Plan your shipping before agreeing to 'not allowed'.

44E / 44F
Ports of loading and discharge

The transport document must show these ports as named. A different port is a discrepancy.

44C
Latest date of shipment

The on-board date cannot be later than this. Unlike the expiry date, it is not extended for a bank holiday.

45A
Description of goods

The invoice description must correspond with this field (Art. (c)). Reproducing it in full is the safest practice. A mirror image isn't required, but omitting or changing details invites a discrepancy. Other documents may describe the goods in general terms that do not conflict.

46A
Documents required

List every document, the number of originals and copies, and who must issue or sign each one.

47A
Additional conditions

Where most surprises sit. A condition with no document to show compliance is disregarded, but every documentary condition must be met.

71D
Charges

Who pays bank charges outside the issuing bank. Factor these into your price.

48
Period for presentation

Days after shipment to present documents. If blank, 21 calendar days apply, always within expiry.

49
Confirmation instructions

CONFIRM, MAY ADD or WITHOUT. If you need confirmation, arrange it before you ship.

What to check on each document

UCP 600 · Art.

Commercial invoice

  • Appears to be issued by the beneficiary
  • Made out in the name of the applicant
  • Same currency as the credit
  • Goods description corresponds with the LC
  • Need not be signed unless the LC asks
UCP 600 · Art. &

Bill of lading

  • Names the carrier and is signed by the carrier, master or a named agent
  • Shows goods shipped on board, with a date
  • Port of loading and discharge as stated in the LC
  • Full set of originals, if issued in more than one
  • Clean: no clause showing defective goods or packing
UCP 600 · Art.

Insurance document

  • Issued and signed by an insurer, underwriter or their agent
  • Dated no later than shipment, unless cover starts from shipment
  • At least 110% of the CIF or CIP value if the LC is silent
  • Same currency as the credit
  • All originals presented if more than one was issued
UCP 600 · Art. &

Draft, origin certificate and others

  • Draft must not be drawn on the applicant
  • Amount within the credit amount and tolerance
  • Certificate issued by the party the LC names
  • Data does not conflict with other documents
  • Content appears to fulfil the document's function

Where presentations usually go wrong

Late presentation

Documents presented after the presentation period or the expiry date. Track both dates from the day of shipment.

Amount over the credit

Invoice or draft exceeds the LC amount plus any allowed tolerance.

Goods description mismatch

The invoice description does not correspond with the LC. Other documents may describe goods in general terms, but the invoice must match.

Conflicting data

Weights, marks, or dates differ between documents. Data need not be identical, but it must not conflict.

Claused bill of lading

A notation that goods or packing were damaged makes the transport document unclean.

Missing on-board notation

The bill of lading does not show that goods were actually loaded, or the on-board date is missing.

Insurance dated after shipment

Or the cover is below 110% of the CIF or CIP value, or in a different currency.

Partial or transhipment breach

Goods shipped in parts, or transhipped, when the LC prohibits it.

Missing originals

Fewer originals presented than the LC or the document itself says were issued.

Before you present: an exporter's checklist

Most discrepancies are avoidable. These are the checks that save the most trouble.

  • Read the LC the day it arrives

    Ask for amendments before shipment, when the buyer still has a reason to agree.

  • Work backwards from three dates

    Latest shipment (44C), presentation period (48) and expiry (31D). The earliest limit is the one that counts.

  • Reproduce the goods description in full

    The invoice must correspond with 45A. Copying it in full is the safest approach. It need not be a mirror image, but omitted or altered details are one of the most common reasons for refusal.

  • Keep data consistent across documents

    Quantities, weights, marks and LC number should not conflict between invoice, packing list and transport document.

  • Match names, addresses and ports

    Use the names and ports exactly as the LC states them.

  • Count the originals

    Present the number of originals 46A asks for, and the full set of any bill of lading issued in more than one original.

  • Check signatures and endorsements

    Bills of lading made out to order and insurance documents usually need endorsing.

  • Read 47A twice

    Documentary conditions there are as binding as 46A. Note any special requirements on certificates or wording.

  • Present early

    Leave time inside the presentation period to correct a discrepancy and present again.
