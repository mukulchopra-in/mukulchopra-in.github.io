Glossary

Quick definitions of the terms used across these pages.

Advising bank
Passes the LC to the beneficiary after checking it is genuine.
Applicant
The buyer who asks its bank to issue the credit.
Back-to-back LC
A second LC issued on the strength of a first one, often used by traders.
Beneficiary
The seller in whose favour the credit is issued.
Complying presentation
Documents that meet the LC terms, UCP 600 and standard banking practice.
Confirming bank
Adds its own payment undertaking on top of the issuing bank's.
D/P and D/A
Documents against payment and documents against acceptance, the two ways a collection releases documents.
Demand guarantee
A bank's undertaking to pay on a complying demand, used as security rather than as payment.
Discrepancy
Any way in which the documents fail to comply with the credit.
Drawee
The party on whom a bill of exchange is drawn, who is asked to pay or accept it.
Electronic record
Data created and transmitted electronically, presented under a credit subject to the eUCP.
Incoterms
ICC trade terms that set out delivery, risk and cost responsibilities between seller and buyer.
Issuing bank
The buyer's bank that issues the LC and gives the main payment undertaking.
MT700
The SWIFT message format used to issue a documentary credit.
Nominated bank
The bank the credit is available with for payment or negotiation.
Reimbursing bank
The bank instructed by the issuing bank to pay a claiming bank's reimbursement claim.
Sight LC
Payment is made as soon as complying documents are presented.
Standby LC
A credit used as a backup guarantee, paid only if the applicant defaults. Often issued under ISP98.
Usance LC
Payment is made at a future date, such as 90 days after shipment.
