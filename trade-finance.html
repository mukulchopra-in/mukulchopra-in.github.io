UCP 600 · ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits

Documentary credits

The rules that govern almost every letter of credit worldwide. Summaries here are in my own words; always check the official ICC text for exact wording.

Based on ICC Publication No. 600 · Last reviewed October 2026

What is a letter of credit?

An LC is a bank's promise to pay the seller, provided the seller presents documents that comply with the credit's terms. The bank looks only at the documents, never at the goods. That single idea explains most of how LCs work.

  1. Buyer ↔ SellerSale contractParties agree that payment will be by LC.
  2. Applicant → Issuing bankLC applicationBuyer asks its bank to issue the credit.
  3. Issuing bank → Advising bankIssuanceLC is sent, usually by SWIFT MT700.
  4. Advising bank → BeneficiaryAdviceSeller's bank checks authenticity and advises it.
  5. BeneficiaryShipmentSeller ships goods and gathers the documents.
  6. Beneficiary → Nominated bankPresentationDocuments are presented within the period and expiry.
  7. BanksExaminationUp to 5 banking days to decide if documents comply.
  8. Issuing bank → BeneficiaryPaymentComplying documents are honoured; buyer reimburses.
ApplicantThe buyer who requests the credit.
BeneficiaryThe seller who gets paid.
Issuing bankGives the main promise to pay.
Advising bankChecks authenticity and passes the LC on.
Confirming bankAdds its own promise to pay.
Nominated bankAuthorised to pay, accept or negotiate.

Reading an MT700

An LC usually arrives as a SWIFT MT700. These are the fields I check first, and what to look for in each.

What to check on each document

UCP 600 · Art. 18

Commercial invoice

  • Appears to be issued by the beneficiary
  • Made out in the name of the applicant
  • Same currency as the credit
  • Goods description corresponds with the LC
  • Need not be signed unless the LC asks
UCP 600 · Art. 20 & 27

Bill of lading

  • Names the carrier and is signed by the carrier, master or a named agent
  • Shows goods shipped on board, with a date
  • Port of loading and discharge as stated in the LC
  • Full set of originals, if issued in more than one
  • Clean: no clause showing defective goods or packing
UCP 600 · Art. 28

Insurance document

  • Issued and signed by an insurer, underwriter or their agent
  • Dated no later than shipment, unless cover starts from shipment
  • At least 110% of the CIF or CIP value if the LC is silent
  • Same currency as the credit
  • All originals presented if more than one was issued
UCP 600 · Art. 6 & 14

Draft, origin certificate and others

  • Draft must not be drawn on the applicant
  • Amount within the credit amount and tolerance
  • Certificate issued by the party the LC names
  • Data does not conflict with other documents
  • Content appears to fulfil the document's function

    ISBP 821 · International Standard Banking Practice

    Examination practice

    UCP 600 says documents must comply with "international standard banking practice". ISBP is where the ICC writes that practice down, document by document.

    Based on ICC Publication No. 821 · Last reviewed October 2026

    What it is

    A companion to UCP 600, published by the ICC Banking Commission. ISBP 821 replaced ISBP 745. It does not change UCP 600; it explains how examiners apply it.

    How it is organised

    General principles first, then a section for each document: drafts, invoices, transport documents, insurance, certificates of origin, packing and weight lists, beneficiary's certificates and inspection certificates.

    URDG 758 · Uniform Rules for Demand Guarantees

    Demand guarantees

    Rules for guarantees and counter-guarantees payable on a complying demand, such as performance, advance payment, bid and retention guarantees.

    Based on ICC Publication No. 758 · Last reviewed October 2026

    Guarantee or letter of credit?

    Documentary credit (UCP 600)Demand guarantee (URDG 758)
    PurposePrimary means of paymentSecurity if the applicant does not perform
    Expected outcomeBank pays in the normal courseBank pays only if something goes wrong
    Who presentsSeller, with shipping documentsBeneficiary, with a demand and supporting statement
    Examination time5 banking days5 business days

    In India, most bank guarantees are issued under Indian law in the bank's own format. URDG 758 applies only when the guarantee expressly says it is subject to it, which is common in international guarantees.

      URC 522 · Uniform Rules for Collections

      Documentary collections

      Banks pass documents from seller to buyer and release them against payment or acceptance. Unlike an LC, no bank promises to pay.

      Based on ICC Publication No. 522 · Last reviewed October 2026

      Two ways to release documents

      D/P

      Documents against payment

      The buyer gets the documents, and so the goods, only after paying. Usually at sight.

      D/A

      Documents against acceptance

      The buyer gets the documents after accepting a bill of exchange to pay at a future date. The seller carries the credit risk until then.

      The parties

      PrincipalThe seller who hands documents to its bank.
      Remitting bankThe seller's bank that sends the collection.
      Collecting bankAny bank, other than the remitting bank, involved in processing.
      Presenting bankThe collecting bank that presents to the buyer.
      DraweeThe buyer, to whom documents are presented.

        URR 725 · Uniform Rules for Bank-to-Bank Reimbursements

        Bank reimbursements

        When a nominated bank pays under an LC, it often claims its money from a third bank, not the issuing bank. URR 725 governs that claim.

        Based on ICC Publication No. 725 · Last reviewed October 2026

        1. Issuing bank → Reimbursing bankAuthorisationIssuing bank authorises the reimbursing bank to pay claims under the credit.
        2. Claiming bank → Reimbursing bankClaimAfter paying the beneficiary, the nominated bank sends its claim.
        3. Reimbursing bank → Claiming bankPaymentReimbursing bank pays and debits the issuing bank.

        Incoterms® 2020 · ICC rules for the use of trade terms

        Delivery terms

        Eleven three-letter terms that settle who arranges transport and insurance, who clears customs, and where risk moves from seller to buyer. The term chosen shapes which documents the LC asks for.

        Based on ICC Incoterms® 2020 · Last reviewed October 2026

        7 terms

        Any mode of transport

        EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP. Use these for containers and multimodal shipments.

        4 terms

        Sea and inland waterway only

        FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF. Best for bulk cargo loaded directly onto a vessel.

        ISP98 · International Standby Practices

        Standby credits

        A standby LC works like a guarantee: the bank pays only if the applicant fails to perform. UCP 600 can apply to standbys, but ISP98 was written specifically for them.

        Based on ICC Publication No. 590 · Last reviewed October 2026

        Where standbys are used

        Common in the US and in cross-border lending, where banks there have traditionally issued standbys instead of guarantees. Used to back loan repayment, performance, advance payments and trade payables.

        Why separate rules

        Many UCP 600 articles deal with shipping documents that a standby never sees. ISP98 deals directly with demands, statements of default and the realities of standby practice.

        eUCP Version 2.1 · Supplement to UCP 600

        Electronic presentation

        The eUCP adds rules for presenting electronic records, alone or together with paper documents, under a credit subject to UCP 600.

        Based on the ICC eUCP Version 2.1 · Last reviewed October 2026

        How it works with UCP 600

        The credit must state that it is subject to the eUCP. UCP 600 still applies, and the eUCP adds or adjusts rules where electronic records are involved. Where they conflict, the eUCP prevails.

        Why it matters

        Electronic bills of lading and digital trade platforms are growing. Banks and exporters need clear rules on when an electronic presentation is complete and what happens if a record cannot be read.
