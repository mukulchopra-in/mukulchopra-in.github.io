ISP98 · International Standby Practices

Standby credits

A standby LC works like a guarantee: the bank pays only if the applicant fails to perform. UCP 600 can apply to standbys, but ISP98 was written specifically for them.

Based on ICC Publication No. 590 · Last reviewed October 2026

Where standbys are used

Common in the US and in cross-border lending, where banks there have traditionally issued standbys instead of guarantees. Used to back loan repayment, performance, advance payments and trade payables.

Why separate rules

Many UCP 600 articles deal with shipping documents that a standby never sees. ISP98 deals directly with demands, statements of default and the realities of standby practice.

Key provisions

Rule 1.06

Nature of standbys

A standby is irrevocable, independent of the underlying transaction, documentary, and binding from the moment it is issued.

Rule 3.13

Expiry on a non-business day

If the last day for presentation falls on a day the place for presentation is closed, a presentation on the next business day is in time.

Rule 3.14

Closure on a business day

If the place for presentation is closed for reasons such as force majeure on the last business day, the last day for presentation is extended to 30 calendar days after the place reopens, unless the standby says otherwise.

Rule 4.16

Demand for payment

A demand need not be separate from the beneficiary's statement or other required document, and need not be in the form of a draft.

Rule 5.01

Time to give notice of dishonour

Notice must be given within a time that is not unreasonable after presentation. Up to three business days is deemed not unreasonable; more than seven business days is deemed unreasonable.

Rule 6.02

Transfer

A standby is not transferable unless it says so. If transferable, it may be transferred in its entirety more than once, but not partially.
