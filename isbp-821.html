ISBP 821 · International Standard Banking Practice

Examination practice

UCP 600 says documents must comply with "international standard banking practice". ISBP is where the ICC writes that practice down, document by document.

Based on ICC Publication No. 821 · Last reviewed October 2026

What it is

A companion to UCP 600, published by the ICC Banking Commission. ISBP 821 replaced ISBP 745. It does not change UCP 600; it explains how examiners apply it.

How it is organised

General principles first, then a section for each document: drafts, invoices, transport documents, insurance, certificates of origin, packing and weight lists, beneficiary's certificates and inspection certificates.

Practice points examiners use every day

General

Read the credit, the document and ISBP together

Data in a document is examined in the context of the credit, the document itself and standard practice. Examiners do not need to be experts in the goods.

General

Typing errors that do not change meaning are fine

A misspelling or typing error that does not affect the meaning of a word or sentence does not make a document discrepant.

General

Abbreviations are generally acceptable

Commonly used abbreviations, such as 'Ltd' for Limited or 'Int'l' for International, may be used in place of the full word.

General

Language

If the credit names a language for documents, the data must be in that language. If it is silent, documents may be issued in any language.

Drafts

Amount in words and figures

The amount in words should match the amount in figures. Where they conflict, the amount in words is treated as the amount demanded.

Invoices

No goods that the credit does not call for

An invoice must not show goods not called for by the credit, including samples or advertising material, even if marked free of charge.

Invoices

Description must correspond

The invoice goods description must correspond with the credit. It does not need to be a mirror image, but it must not conflict or describe different goods.

Transport documents

On-board notation

Where a bill of lading uses a pre-printed 'shipped on board' clause, the issue date is the shipment date. A separate dated on-board notation, where present, sets the shipment date.

Insurance

Cover for the right risks and amount

The insurance document must cover the risks the credit names, in the credit's currency, and for at least the required percentage of value.

Certificates

Issuer and function

A certificate of origin, inspection or analysis certificate must be issued by the party the credit names, relate to the invoiced goods, and appear to fulfil its function.
