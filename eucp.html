eUCP Version 2.1 · Supplement to UCP 600

Electronic presentation

The eUCP adds rules for presenting electronic records, alone or together with paper documents, under a credit subject to UCP 600.

Based on the ICC eUCP Version 2.1 · Last reviewed October 2026

How it works with UCP 600

The credit must state that it is subject to the eUCP. UCP 600 still applies, and the eUCP adds or adjusts rules where electronic records are involved. Where they conflict, the eUCP prevails.

Why it matters

Electronic bills of lading and digital trade platforms are growing. Banks and exporters need clear rules on when an electronic presentation is complete and what happens if a record cannot be read.

Key points

Scope

Paper, electronic or both

The eUCP covers presentations made wholly by electronic records or by a mix of electronic records and paper documents.

Credit terms

Format and place must be stated

A credit subject to the eUCP should state the format of each electronic record and the place for presenting electronic records. If a format is not stated, any format may be used.

Presentation

Notice of completeness

Electronic records may be sent separately, but the beneficiary must give a notice of completeness. The presentation is not treated as made until that notice is received.

Examination

When the clock starts

The time for examination starts on the banking day following the day the notice of completeness is received.

Corrupted records

Re-presentation

If a record appears corrupted, the bank may ask for it to be presented again. The examination time is suspended until it is, and if it is not re-presented within the stated time, it is treated as not presented.

Originals and copies

No separate original

Presenting one electronic record satisfies a requirement for one or more originals or copies of that record.
