URDG 758 · Uniform Rules for Demand Guarantees

Demand guarantees

Rules for guarantees and counter-guarantees payable on a complying demand, such as performance, advance payment, bid and retention guarantees.

Based on ICC Publication No. 758 · Last reviewed October 2026

Guarantee or letter of credit?

In India, most bank guarantees are issued under Indian law in the bank's own format. URDG 758 applies only when the guarantee expressly says it is subject to it, which is common in international guarantees.

All 35 articles

General provisions

Articles 1–7
Art. 1Application of URDG
The rules apply to a demand guarantee or counter-guarantee that expressly says it is subject to them, and bind all parties except where the guarantee changes or excludes them. Where a guarantee is issued subject to URDG at a counter-guarantor's request, the counter-guarantee is also subject to URDG unless it says otherwise. A guarantee issued on or after 1 July 2010 that names URDG without a version is subject to URDG 758.
Art. 2Definitions
Defines the key terms: applicant, beneficiary, guarantor, counter-guarantor, instructing party, advising party, business day, demand, complying demand, presentation, supporting statement, expiry, expiry date and expiry event, among others. A business day is a day the place of business where the act is to be done is regularly open.
Art. 3Interpretation
Branches of a guarantor in different countries count as separate entities. A requirement for originals or copies of an electronic document is met by one electronic document. In dates, "from", "to", "until", "till" and "between" include the date mentioned; "before" and "after" exclude it. Vague words such as "first class" or "competent" allow any issuer except the beneficiary or the applicant.
Art. 4Issue and effectiveness
A guarantee is issued when it leaves the guarantor's control. It is irrevocable once issued, even if it does not say so. The beneficiary can present a demand from the date of issue, or from any later date or event the guarantee specifies.
Art. 5Independence of guarantee and counter-guarantee
A guarantee is independent of the underlying contract and of the application to issue it; the guarantor is not concerned with or bound by them. Referring to the underlying contract for identification does not change this. The guarantor's promise to pay cannot be defeated by claims or defences arising from any relationship other than its own with the beneficiary. A counter-guarantee is likewise independent of the guarantee it supports.
Art. 6Documents v. goods, services or performance
Guarantors deal with documents, not with the goods, services or performance the documents relate to.
Art. 7Non-documentary conditions
A guarantee should not contain a condition, other than a date or a period, without stating a document that shows the condition has been met. If no document is stated and the guarantor cannot confirm the condition from its own records or an index named in the guarantee, the condition is disregarded.

Instructions, issue, advising and amendments

Articles 8–11
Art. 8Content of instructions and guarantees
Instructions and guarantees should be clear and precise and avoid excessive detail. A guarantee should state the applicant, beneficiary and guarantor, the underlying relationship, the maximum amount and currency, the expiry, the terms for demanding payment, the form and language of documents, and who pays charges.
Art. 9Application not taken up
If a guarantor is not prepared or not able to issue a guarantee when it receives the application, it should tell the party that gave the instructions without delay.
Art. 10Advising of guarantee or amendment
A guarantee can be advised to the beneficiary through an advising party. By advising it, that party confirms it is satisfied the guarantee appears authentic and that the advice reflects its terms accurately; it takes on no payment obligation. A party that will not advise, or cannot check authenticity, must say so without delay.
Art. 11Amendments
An amendment does not bind the beneficiary without its agreement, but binds the guarantor from the moment it is issued unless and until the beneficiary rejects it. Partial acceptance of an amendment counts as rejection. A clause saying an amendment takes effect unless rejected within a set time is disregarded. The guarantor informs the instructing party of the beneficiary's acceptance or rejection without delay.

Liability and amount

Articles 12–13
Art. 12Extent of guarantor's liability
The guarantor is liable to the beneficiary only under the terms of the guarantee and these rules, and only up to the guarantee amount.
Art. 13Variation of amount of guarantee
A guarantee may provide for its amount to reduce or increase on specified dates or events. Where an event triggers the change, it is treated as happening only when the document stated in the guarantee is presented, or when the guarantor can confirm it from its own records or an index named in the guarantee.

Demands and examination

Articles 14–24
Art. 14Presentation
A demand must be presented to the guarantor at the place of issue or another place stated in the guarantee, on or before expiry. A presentation can say it will be completed later, but must be completed before expiry. Electronic presentation is possible only if the guarantee allows it; otherwise presentation is on paper. Each presentation should identify the guarantee it relates to.
Art. 15Requirements for demand
A demand must come with any documents the guarantee requires and, in any case, a supporting statement from the beneficiary saying in what respect the applicant is in breach of the underlying contract. Under a counter-guarantee, the demand must be supported by a statement that a complying demand has been received under the guarantee. These statements are required unless the guarantee expressly excludes them.
Art. 16Information about demand
The guarantor must inform the instructing party, or the counter-guarantor, without delay of any demand and of any request to extend the expiry instead of paying.
Art. 17Partial and multiple demands
A demand may be for less than the full amount, and more than one demand may be made unless the guarantee says otherwise. A demand for more than the amount available is not complying. If only one demand is allowed and it is rejected, the beneficiary may still present another demand before expiry.
Art. 18Separateness of each demand
Making a non-complying demand, or withdrawing a demand, does not stop the beneficiary making another timely demand. Paying a non-complying demand does not waive the requirement that other demands must comply.
Art. 19Examination
The guarantor decides, on the basis of the presentation alone, whether a demand appears on its face to be complying. Data in the demand and documents need not be identical but must not conflict with each other or the guarantee. Documents the guarantee does not ask for are disregarded and may be returned.
Art. 20Time for examination of demand; payment
The guarantor has five business days following the day of presentation to examine a demand and decide whether it complies. Expiry after presentation does not shorten this time. If the demand complies, the guarantor must pay.
Art. 21Currency of payment
Payment is made in the currency of the guarantee. If the guarantor cannot pay in that currency because of an impediment beyond its control, it pays in the currency of the place of payment.
Art. 22Transmission of copies of complying demand
The guarantor must send a copy of the complying demand and related documents to the instructing party, or the counter-guarantor, without delay.
Art. 23Extend or pay
Where a complying demand asks, as an alternative, for the expiry to be extended, the guarantor may suspend payment for up to 30 calendar days following receipt of the demand while the parties decide. If the extension is granted, the demand is treated as withdrawn. If it is not granted, the demand is paid without a further demand. The guarantor may refuse to extend even if instructed to, and then pays.
Art. 24Non-complying demand, waiver and notice
A guarantor may reject a non-complying demand. It may ask the instructing party for a waiver, but this does not extend the examination time. A rejection must be a single notice, given no later than the close of the fifth business day following the day of presentation, listing each discrepancy. A guarantor that fails to do this cannot later claim the demand did not comply.

Reduction, termination and force majeure

Articles 25–26
Art. 25Reduction and termination
The guarantee amount is reduced by payments made, partial releases and any reduction clause. A guarantee terminates on expiry, when nothing more is payable, or when the beneficiary presents a signed release. If a guarantee states neither an expiry date nor an expiry event, it terminates three years after issue, and a counter-guarantee terminates 30 calendar days after its guarantee. An expiry date falling on a non-business day at the place of presentation moves to the next business day.
Art. 26Force majeure
If force majeure prevents presentation or payment, a guarantee that would have expired during that time is extended by 30 calendar days from its original expiry. Examination of a demand already presented is suspended until the guarantor reopens, and a complying demand that could not be paid is paid once the force majeure ends.

Disclaimers, indemnity and charges

Articles 27–32
Art. 27Disclaimer on effectiveness of documents
Guarantors are not responsible for the form, accuracy, genuineness or legal effect of any document presented, nor for the good faith or acts of anyone who issued it.
Art. 28Disclaimer on transmission and translation
Guarantors are not liable for delay, loss or errors in transmitting messages or documents, nor for errors in translation.
Art. 29Disclaimer for acts of another party
A guarantor that uses another party to carry out the applicant's instructions does so for the applicant's account and risk.
Art. 30Limits on exemption from liability
None of the disclaimers protect a guarantor from liability for failing to act in good faith.
Art. 31Indemnity for foreign laws and usages
The applicant must indemnify the guarantor, and the counter-guarantor must indemnify the guarantor, against obligations imposed by foreign laws and usages.
Art. 32Liability for charges
A party that asks another to perform services is liable for that party's charges. If the guarantee says charges are for the beneficiary and they cannot be collected, the instructing party remains liable. A guarantee should not make payment conditional on charges being paid.

Transfer, governing law and jurisdiction

Articles 33–35
Art. 33Transfer of guarantee and assignment of proceeds
A guarantee can be transferred only if it expressly says it is transferable, and then only for the full amount available at the time; it may be transferred more than once. A counter-guarantee is not transferable. The beneficiary may assign proceeds under applicable law, but the guarantor need not pay an assignee unless it has agreed.
Art. 34Governing law
Unless the guarantee says otherwise, it is governed by the law of the place of the guarantor's branch or office that issued it. A counter-guarantee is governed by the law of the place of the counter-guarantor's issuing branch or office.
Art. 35Jurisdiction
Unless the guarantee says otherwise, disputes go to the competent court of the country where the guarantor's issuing branch or office is located; for a counter-guarantee, the country of the counter-guarantor's issuing branch or office.

Art. 15

Drafting, presenting and handling demands

Small drafting choices decide how a guarantee behaves when a demand arrives.

  • Check the rules clause

    If you expect URDG 758 to apply, make sure the guarantee says it is subject to URDG 758. Otherwise local law and the bank's own wording apply.

    Art. 1
  • Always set an expiry

    Give a clear expiry date or a documented expiry event. Without one, a URDG guarantee runs for three years.

    Art. 25
  • Keep the wording independent

    Refer to the contract only to identify it. Copying contract terms into the guarantee adds excessive detail and invites disputes.

    Art. 5, 8
  • Avoid non-documentary conditions

    If a condition matters, name the document that proves it. Otherwise the guarantor will disregard it.

    Art. 7
  • Include the supporting statement

    A beneficiary's demand must say in what respect the applicant is in breach, unless the guarantee excludes this.

    Art. 15
  • Present early, at the right place

    Present at the place stated, before expiry, and leave a margin. A presentation to be completed later must still be completed before expiry.

    Art. 14
  • Plan for extend or pay

    Applicants should be ready to decide quickly. The guarantor can hold payment for at most 30 calendar days.

    Art. 23
  • Reject properly, or not at all

    Guarantors: send one notice listing every discrepancy within five business days, or lose the right to reject.

    Art. 24
  • Use reduction clauses

    For advance payment guarantees, tie reductions to documents such as shipment or milestone certificates.

    Art. 13
