URC 522 · Uniform Rules for Collections

Documentary collections

Banks pass documents from seller to buyer and release them against payment or acceptance. Unlike an LC, no bank promises to pay.

Based on ICC Publication No. 522 · Last reviewed October 2026

Two ways to release documents

D/P

Documents against payment

The bank releases the documents to the buyer only against payment, usually at sight. This protects the seller only if those documents give control of the goods, such as a full set of order bills of lading.

D/A

Documents against acceptance

The buyer gets the documents after accepting a bill of exchange to pay at a future date. The seller carries the credit risk until then.

The parties

PrincipalThe seller who hands documents to its bank.
Remitting bankThe seller's bank that sends the collection.
Collecting bankAny bank, other than the remitting bank, involved in processing.
Presenting bankThe collecting bank that presents to the buyer.
DraweeThe buyer, to whom documents are presented.

All 26 articles

General provisions and definitions

Articles 1–4
Art. 1Application of URC 522
The rules apply to every collection whose collection instruction says it is subject to URC 522, and bind all parties unless they agree otherwise or local law says otherwise. A bank does not have to handle a collection, but if it decides not to, it must tell the party that sent it without delay.
Art. 2Definition of collection
A collection is the handling of documents by banks, following instructions, to obtain payment or acceptance, or to deliver documents against payment, acceptance or other terms. Financial documents are bills of exchange, promissory notes, cheques and similar; commercial documents are invoices, transport documents and the like. A clean collection covers financial documents alone. A documentary collection covers financial documents with commercial documents, or commercial documents without any financial document.
Art. 3Parties to a collection
The principal (usually the exporter) entrusts the collection to its bank. The remitting bank sends it on. A collecting bank is any bank other than the remitting bank that handles it. The presenting bank is the collecting bank that presents the documents to the drawee, the party (usually the buyer) to whom presentation is made.
Art. 4Collection instruction
Every set of documents must travel with a collection instruction that says it is subject to URC 522 and gives complete and precise instructions. Banks act only on those instructions and do not look into the documents to find instructions. The instruction should cover the parties, the drawee's full address, amount and currency, documents enclosed, terms of payment or acceptance, charges, interest, method of payment and what to do on non-payment or non-acceptance.

Form and structure of collections

Articles 5–8
Art. 5Presentation
Presentation means making the documents available to the drawee as instructed. Documents go to the drawee in the form received, though banks may add stamps and endorsements needed for the collection. If the principal names a collecting bank, the remitting bank uses it; otherwise it may choose one.
Art. 6Sight and acceptance
For documents payable at sight, the presenting bank presents for payment without delay. For documents payable at a later date, it presents for acceptance without delay where acceptance is called for, and presents for payment no later than the maturity date.
Art. 7Release of commercial documents
A collection should not include a bill of exchange payable at a future date together with an instruction to release documents against payment. If it does, the instruction must say clearly whether documents are released against acceptance (D/A) or payment (D/P). If it does not say, documents are released only against payment, and the collecting bank is not responsible for any delay this causes.
Art. 8Creation of documents
If the remitting bank asks the collecting bank or the drawee to create documents that are not in the collection, such as bills of exchange, trust receipts or letters of undertaking, the remitting bank must supply their form and wording. Otherwise the collecting bank is not responsible for the documents created.

Liabilities and responsibilities

Articles 9–15
Art. 9Good faith and reasonable care
Banks must act in good faith and exercise reasonable care.
Art. 10Documents v. goods, services or performances
Goods should not be sent directly to a bank or consigned to its order without the bank first agreeing. If they are, the bank has no duty to take delivery and the goods stay at the sender's risk. Banks have no duty to protect the goods; if they do act to protect them, they take no liability, and any costs fall on the party that sent the collection.
Art. 11Disclaimer for acts of an instructed party
A bank that uses another bank to carry out the principal's instructions does so for the principal's account and risk, even if it chose that other bank itself. The party giving instructions must indemnify the banks against obligations imposed by foreign laws and usages.
Art. 12Disclaimer on documents received
Banks must check only that the documents received appear to be as listed in the collection instruction, and must tell the sender without delay if any are missing or different. They have no further duty to examine the documents. If the remitting bank does not hear of a difference, it cannot later dispute what the collecting bank received.
Art. 13Disclaimer on effectiveness of documents
Banks are not responsible for the form, accuracy, genuineness or legal effect of any document, nor for the description, quantity, condition or value of the goods, nor for the good faith or acts of anyone else involved.
Art. 14Disclaimer on delays, loss in transit and translation
Banks are not liable for delay or loss of messages, letters or documents in transit, nor for translation errors. They are also not liable for delays caused by the need to get clarification of instructions they have received.
Art. 15Force majeure
Banks are not liable for consequences of interruptions to their business caused by events beyond their control, such as natural disasters, riots, war, terrorism or strikes.

Payment

Articles 16–19
Art. 16Payment without delay
Amounts collected, less charges and expenses where applicable, must be made available without delay to the party that sent the collection instruction. Unless otherwise agreed, the collecting bank pays only the remitting bank.
Art. 17Payment in local currency
For documents payable in the currency of the country of payment, the presenting bank releases documents against payment in that currency only if it can be used immediately as the collection instruction specifies, unless instructed otherwise.
Art. 18Payment in foreign currency
For documents payable in another currency, the presenting bank releases documents against payment in that currency only if it can be remitted immediately as instructed, unless instructed otherwise.
Art. 19Partial payments
In a clean collection, partial payments may be accepted where the law allows, but the financial documents are released only once paid in full. In a documentary collection, partial payments are accepted only if the instruction specifically allows them, and documents are released only after full payment, unless instructed otherwise.

Interest, charges and expenses

Articles 20–21
Art. 20Interest
If interest is to be collected and the drawee refuses to pay it, the presenting bank may still release the documents against payment or acceptance without the interest, unless the instruction expressly says interest may not be waived. The instruction should state the rate, period and basis. If interest cannot be waived and the drawee refuses, the bank does not release the documents and informs the bank it received them from.
Art. 21Charges and expenses
If charges are for the drawee's account and the drawee refuses them, the presenting bank may release documents without collecting them, unless the instruction expressly says charges may not be waived. Waived charges fall on the principal and may be deducted from the proceeds. Banks may ask for advance payment of expected expenses.

Other provisions

Articles 22–26
Art. 22Acceptance
The presenting bank must see that the form of acceptance on a bill of exchange appears complete and correct. It is not responsible for the genuineness of signatures or the authority of the person signing.
Art. 23Promissory notes and other instruments
The presenting bank is not responsible for the genuineness of any signature, or the authority of any signatory, on a promissory note, receipt or other instrument.
Art. 24Protest
The collection instruction should say whether to protest, or take other legal steps, on non-payment or non-acceptance. Without such instructions, banks have no duty to protest. Any protest fees are for the principal's account.
Art. 25Case-of-need
If the principal names a representative to act if payment or acceptance is refused, the collection instruction should state that person's powers clearly. Without that, banks will not accept instructions from the case-of-need.
Art. 26Advices
The collecting bank must advise payment, acceptance, non-payment or non-acceptance without delay, quoting the remitting bank's reference. On non-payment or non-acceptance, the presenting bank should try to find out why and report it. If it receives no instructions within 60 days of that advice, it may return the documents to the bank it received them from, with no further responsibility.

Key points to remember

The rules that settle most questions on a collection.

if a usance bill's terms are unclear

Where a collection includes a bill payable at a future date and the instruction does not say D/A or D/P, documents are released only against payment. The collecting bank is not responsible for any delay this causes.

Art. 7
No
examination

Banks only check that the documents received match the list in the collection instruction. They do not examine what the documents say.

In a documentary collection, documents are released only after full payment, unless partial payments are specifically allowed.

The presenting bank may release documents without collecting interest or charges, unless the instruction says they may not be waived.

If no instructions arrive within 60 days of an advice of non-payment or non-acceptance, the documents may be sent back.

Without clear instructions, banks have no duty to protest an unpaid or unaccepted bill.

Art. 24

Before you send a collection

Most collection problems start with an unclear instruction. These checks avoid them.

  • Say D/P or D/A, clearly

    State the release terms in the collection instruction. Avoid sending a usance bill with a D/P instruction; if you must, say so expressly.

    Art. 7
  • Make sure the documents control the goods

    D/P protects you only if the buyer needs the documents to get the goods. Use a full set of order bills of lading. With an air waybill or sea waybill consigned to the buyer, the buyer may be able to collect the goods without paying.

  • Give the drawee's full address

    Include the complete postal address, phone and email. Banks are not liable for delays caused by an incomplete address.

    Art. 4
  • Never consign goods to the bank

    Don't ship goods to the collecting bank or to its order without its prior agreement. The bank has no duty to take delivery, and the goods stay at your risk.

    Art. 10
  • Decide on interest and charges

    If interest or charges must not be waived, say so expressly. Otherwise the presenting bank may release documents without collecting them.

    Art. 20, 21
  • Give protest instructions

    If you want an unpaid or unaccepted bill protested, say so. Without instructions, banks have no duty to protest.

    Art. 24
  • Name a case-of-need, or none

    A local representative can help if the buyer refuses, but only if the instruction states their powers clearly.

    Art. 25
  • Respond to non-payment advices

    When the bank reports non-payment or non-acceptance, send instructions promptly. After 60 days without them, documents may come back.

    Art. 26
  • Know your buyer

    In a collection no bank promises to pay. Check the buyer's standing, and consider export credit insurance for D/A terms.

  • Track export realisation

    Keep an eye on the deadlines for realising export proceeds under RBI rules, and follow up overdue bills with your bank.
